How an Indian electricity bill is actually built

Almost nobody is charged a single flat rate per unit. Your bill is assembled from four or five separate components, and understanding which one is growing tells you whether the fix is behavioural, contractual or structural.

1. Energy charge (the slabs)

This is consumption multiplied by a rate that steps up as you use more. Two structures exist in India and they behave very differently:

  • Telescopic — each slab rate applies only to the units inside that slab. Consuming 250 units in Maharashtra means the first 100 are billed at the lowest rate and only the next 150 at the higher one. Most states work this way.
  • Flat (non-telescopic) — the slab your total lands in sets one rate for every unit you consumed. Telangana and Kerala below 250 units use this. Crossing a boundary by a single unit can raise the rate on your entire consumption, which is why a 201st unit occasionally costs someone several hundred rupees.

2. Fixed or demand charge

Levied per kilowatt of sanctioned load per month — not on what you consume. It is charged even in a month you are travelling and use nothing. People who upgraded their sanctioned load years ago for an air conditioner they no longer own are quietly paying for it every month. If your consumption never approaches your sanctioned load, applying for a load reduction is one of the few genuinely free savings available.

3. Fuel and power purchase cost adjustment (FPPCA / FAC / PPAC)

A pass-through of what the DISCOM paid for power, usually expressed as a percentage of the energy charge and revised quarterly or monthly. In Delhi it has run at roughly a third of the energy charge — a material number most consumers never look at. It is the single most volatile line on your bill and the one most likely to be slightly out of date in any calculator, including this one.

4. Electricity duty and taxes

A state levy on the sum of energy and fixed charges. It ranges from zero in several states to 16% in Maharashtra. Nothing you do changes it.

5. Subsidy adjustments

Several states now zero out part of the bill for eligible households: 100% up to 200 units in Delhi, up to 200 units under Gruha Jyoti in Karnataka and Gruha Jyothi in Telangana, 300 units in Punjab, the first 100 units free in Tamil Nadu, 200 units in Jharkhand. These are applied after the calculation above and usually require opting in. This calculator shows the pre-subsidy figure, which is also the number that matters when you are evaluating solar — because a subsidy can be withdrawn and an installed system cannot.

The effective rate is the number that matters

When you are deciding on rooftop solar, an air conditioner upgrade or an EV, the relevant figure is not your slab rate — it is your effective rate: total bill divided by total units, with every charge included. It is nearly always higher than the headline slab rate people quote, and it is the correct input for any savings calculation. That is the figure this calculator puts in bold, and the one our solar calculator uses by default.
