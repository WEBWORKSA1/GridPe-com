What this model assumes — and why

A savings figure is only as honest as the assumptions behind it. Here is every one we use, so you can argue with them.

Generation3.3–4.9 kWh/kW/dayVaries by state. Rajasthan and Gujarat sit at the top, the north-east at the bottom. Long-run average for an unshaded, well-oriented array.
Panel degradation0.6% / yearStandard warranty curve for mono PERC modules. Year 25 output is roughly 86% of year 1.

The one number to sanity-check a quote

Divide the quoted price by the system size in watts. For a residential grid-tied system with ALMM modules you should be seeing roughly ₹36–70 per watt depending on size — smaller systems cost more per watt because the fixed costs of structure, cabling, net meter and labour spread over fewer kilowatts. A 1 kW quote at ₹65/W is normal; a 10 kW quote at ₹65/W is not.

Where the money actually comes from

Rooftop solar returns are driven by three things, in this order of importance:

  1. Your effective tariff. Every unit you generate displaces a unit you would have bought. At ₹8/unit a kilowatt earns about ₹12,000 a year; at ₹4/unit it earns half that and the payback roughly doubles. This is why solar is a no-brainer in Maharashtra's upper slabs and marginal in a heavily subsidised state.
  2. The subsidy. ₹78,000 on a 3 kW system is roughly 45% of the project cost. It is the single largest lever, it applies only to the first three kilowatts, and it is the reason 3 kW is the most common residential size in India right now.
  3. Local yield. The gap between Rajasthan at 4.8 and West Bengal at 3.6 kWh/kW/day is a 33% difference in output from identical hardware. It changes payback by well over a year.

When rooftop solar does not pay

We would rather you did not spend ₹1.5 lakh on a bad fit. Solar is genuinely weak in these situations:

  • You are in a heavily subsidised slab. If your state gives you 200–300 free units and you consume under that, your effective tariff is near zero and there is nothing to save. Punjab, Karnataka and Telangana households at low consumption frequently fall here.
  • You are renting, or plan to move within four years. The payback is typically 2.5–5 years and the subsidy is tied to the name on the connection. A system you leave behind is a gift to the landlord.
  • Your roof is shaded after 10am. Even partial shading across a string disproportionately cuts output. An overhanging tree or a neighbour's proposed extra floor can destroy the economics.
  • You have under 150 sq ft of clear roof. Below about 1.5 kW, the fixed costs of structure, net meter and labour dominate and per-watt pricing gets punishing.
