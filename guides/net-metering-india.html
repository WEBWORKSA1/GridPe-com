Net metering in India, explained without the jargon

Three different arrangements hide behind the phrase "net metering", and which one your state uses can change a rooftop solar return by a wide margin. Here is what each means for your money.

GPGridPe Research Desk·9 min read·Updated 2026

The basic problem net metering solves

Your panels generate most around midday. Your household consumes most in the evening. Without some arrangement with the grid, the midday surplus would be wasted and the evening demand would be bought at full price. Net metering is the arrangement.

A bidirectional meter records electricity flowing in both directions. Surplus flows out to the grid during the day; you draw from the grid at night. The question that matters financially is how those two flows are valued against each other — and that is where the three models differ.

Model 1: Net metering

Exported units and imported units are simply netted against each other. Export 200 units, import 300, and you are billed for 100 units at your normal tariff. Every exported unit is effectively worth your full retail rate, because it cancels a unit you would otherwise have bought.

This is the most favourable arrangement for a household and the one most people assume they are getting. Surplus that remains at the end of the settlement period — usually the financial year — is typically paid out at a much lower rate, often the DISCOM's average power purchase cost, which is well below retail.

Model 2: Net billing

Imports and exports are valued separately, at different rates. You pay your retail tariff on everything you import. You are credited at a lower, fixed feed-in rate on everything you export. The two are then subtracted in rupees, not units.

The consequence: every unit you export is worth less than every unit you import. The more of your generation you export rather than consume directly, the worse your return. Under net billing, a system sized to your consumption and a household that runs heavy loads in the daytime — washing machine, water pump, EV charging, air conditioning on hot afternoons — do materially better than a system that mostly exports.

Model 3: Gross metering

All generation is exported and credited at a feed-in tariff; all consumption is imported and billed at retail. Your solar and your consumption are financially unrelated. This is the least favourable arrangement for a household with a retail tariff above the feed-in rate, and it is more common for larger installations.

Why this can halve your return

Take a household that consumes 40% of its solar generation directly and exports 60%. Under net metering, all of it is worth the retail rate — say ₹8. Under net billing with a ₹3.50 feed-in rate, the exported 60% is worth less than half as much. The blended value per unit generated drops from ₹8.00 to about ₹5.30, a one-third reduction, and the payback period stretches accordingly.

That is why the GridPe solar calculator credits exported units at 75% of retail rather than assuming a full 1:1 offset — a deliberately conservative middle ground. If you know your state operates true net metering with an annual settlement, your real return will be somewhat better than our estimate. If it operates net billing at a low feed-in rate and you export heavily, it will be worse.

Five things to confirm with your DISCOM before installing

  1. Which model applies to residential rooftop systems of your size, today. Regulations change, and a vendor's answer may be out of date.
  2. The settlement period — monthly, or annual. Annual settlement is better: summer surplus can offset monsoon and winter shortfall.
  3. The rate paid for surplus left at the end of the settlement period.
  4. Any capacity cap relative to your sanctioned load. Many states limit rooftop capacity to a percentage of sanctioned load, which may force a load enhancement.
  5. The net-meter charge and whether the meter is supplied by the DISCOM or procured by you.

How to maximise value under any model

Shift controllable loads into daylight. Run the washing machine, the water pump, the dishwasher and — if you have one — the EV charger between 10am and 3pm. Pre-cool the house on a hot afternoon rather than running the AC hardest at 9pm. Every unit you consume directly from your own panels is worth the full retail rate under every metering model, which makes self-consumption the one strategy that is never wrong.

And size the system to your consumption rather than your roof. Under net billing especially, an oversized system is exporting cheaply for 25 years.

Net-metering regulations are set by each State Electricity Regulatory Commission and are revised periodically. Confirm the current rules with your DISCOM before installing. See our disclaimer.
