Who does what in India's power sector

Three layers sit between a power plant and your meter, and knowing which one you are dealing with saves a great deal of time when something goes wrong.

  • Generation companies (NTPC, state GENCOs, independent producers) make the electricity.
  • Transmission companies (POWERGRID, state TRANSCOs) move it at high voltage across regions.
  • Distribution companies — DISCOMs — take it the last mile, own your meter, issue your bill and handle your connection. This is who you actually deal with.

Above them sit the regulators: the Central Electricity Regulatory Commission for interstate matters and a State Electricity Regulatory Commission in each state, which approves the tariff order that decides what you pay. Grid-India (formerly POSOCO) operates the national grid and publishes the daily demand and frequency data.

What your DISCOM is responsible for

  • New connections, load enhancement and load reduction
  • Meter installation, testing and replacement
  • Billing, and correcting a bill that is wrong
  • Outage restoration in your area
  • Net-metering approval and commissioning for rooftop solar — the step that most often delays a solar installation

Escalating a complaint

The sequence is: the DISCOM's own helpline or app, then the Consumer Grievance Redressal Forum, then the Electricity Ombudsman for your state. Each stage has a statutory response window, and the Ombudsman's decision binds the DISCOM. Keep the complaint number from the first call — the entire escalation path depends on it. For a disputed bill, ask specifically for a meter test report in writing rather than a verbal assurance.

Note that DISCOM names change: several state boards have been unbundled or renamed in recent years, and Telangana's TSSPDCL and TSNPDCL became TGSPDCL and TGNPDCL. If a search returns nothing, try the older name.
