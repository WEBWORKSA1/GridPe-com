Roof Area to Solar Capacity

Total roof area is not usable roof area. Water tanks, staircase headroom, walkways, parapet shadows and the neighbour's building all take a bite. This calculator works from what is genuinely left.

Your roof

70% is typical for an Indian terrace. If your roof is clear and flat with a single tank in a corner, 80–85% is realistic. A cluttered roof with a stair headroom in the middle can drop to 50%.

Judge this between 10am and 3pm, not at breakfast. Anything shaded during those hours is effectively dead area.

Elevated structures change the sum

Panels mounted on a raised structure let you keep using the terrace underneath and can recover some area lost to low obstructions. It adds roughly ₹4,000–8,000 per kW and needs a structural check, but on a small roof with an active terrace it is often the right call.

Rule of thumb

1 kW needs roughly 90 sq ft of clear, unshaded area with standard row spacing. A 550 Wp panel occupies about 27 sq ft. Allow walkway gaps between rows — panels packed edge to edge shade each other in winter, when the sun sits lower.

How to measure a terrace properly

Pace it out or use a tape, and sketch it — do not rely on the builder's plan, which describes the plot, not the usable roof. Then subtract, in this order:

  1. Fixed obstructions. Overhead water tanks, the stair headroom box, lift machine room, dish antennas, existing solar water heaters. Measure their footprint plus about two feet of working clearance.
  2. Access walkways. You need to reach the panels to clean them, and a technician needs to reach the inverter. Allow a metre-wide path.
  3. Parapet shadow. A four-foot parapet casts a real shadow across the north edge in winter. Panels in that band underperform all season.
  4. External shading. Trees, a taller building to the south, a water tank on an adjacent roof. Assess between 10am and 3pm — outside those hours the sun is too low to matter much for output.
Partial shading is worse than it looks

Panels in a string behave somewhat like a series circuit: one heavily shaded module can drag down the output of the entire string, not just its own share. Bypass diodes limit the damage and module-level power electronics limit it further, but the principle holds — a single badly placed shadow can cost far more than the panel area it covers. If shading is unavoidable, ask the installer about micro-inverters or optimisers and price the difference.

Do not fill the roof just because you can

Two constraints cap the useful size regardless of available area. First, the subsidy stops at 3 kW — everything above that is funded entirely by you. Second, and more important, most state net-metering regulations cap sanctioned rooftop capacity in relation to your sanctioned load, and export is credited below retail rate. A system materially larger than your own consumption exports a lot of units at a discounted rate, which stretches payback considerably.

The sweet spot for most Indian homes is a system that generates roughly what you consume, sized from your bill rather than your roof. Use the solar calculator for that number; use this page only to check it actually fits.
