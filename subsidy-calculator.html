How the subsidy is structured

PM Surya Ghar: Muft Bijli Yojana replaced the older per-kW rooftop programme with a flat, banded structure that is deliberately front-loaded towards small residential systems:

  • First 2 kW — ₹30,000 per kW (₹60,000 total)
  • Third kW — ₹18,000
  • Above 3 kW — nothing
  • Hard cap — ₹78,000 per household, regardless of system size

The practical consequence: a 3 kW system attracts ₹78,000 and a 10 kW system attracts the same ₹78,000. Subsidy per kilowatt collapses from ₹30,000 on the first to ₹7,800 averaged over ten. That is why 3 kW has become the default residential size in India — it is the exact point where the subsidy stops subsidising.

Whether you should still go bigger depends entirely on your consumption. If you routinely burn 600 units a month, a 3 kW system covers about two-thirds of it and the extra kilowatts still pay back on their own merits at ₹40,000–48,000/kW unsubsidised — just over a longer horizon. Size to your bill, not to the subsidy cliff.

Housing societies and RWAs

Group Housing Societies and Resident Welfare Associations get a separate line: ₹18,000 per kW for common facilities — lifts, water pumps, corridor lighting, EV charging — up to 500 kW, calculated at 3 kW per house. This is materially underused. A society with a large terrace and a heavy common-area load often has better project economics than any individual flat owner in it, because common-area consumption is steady, daytime, and billed at commercial rates.

The nine conditions that decide eligibility

  1. The applicant must be a residential electricity consumer.
  2. The connection must be in the applicant's name — this is where rented properties fall down.
  3. The system must be grid-connected with a net meter. Off-grid and pure battery-backup systems are not eligible.
  4. The installing vendor must be registered on the National Portal before installation.
  5. Modules and cells must be from the ALMM approved list, with DCR cells.
  6. One subsidy per household connection.
  7. Installation, DISCOM inspection and net-meter commissioning must all be completed and recorded on the portal.
  8. Bank account details on the portal must match the applicant.
  9. The claim must be filed through the portal — money is not released against a vendor's invoice alone.
Timeline reality check

The portal states the subsidy is credited within about 30 days of successful inspection and commissioning. In practice the long poles are DISCOM feasibility approval at the start and net-meter installation at the end — both of which sit with your distribution company, not with the scheme. Budget for the process rather than the headline number, and do not let a vendor tell you the delay is the central government's doing.
