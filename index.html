FAQ

Questions we get constantly

A 3 kW grid-tied system typically lands between ₹1.50 lakh and ₹1.80 lakh installed, with ALMM-listed modules, a standard elevated structure and a net meter. PM Surya Ghar pays ₹78,000 of that — ₹30,000 per kW on the first 2 kW plus ₹18,000 for the third kilowatt — so your net outlay is roughly ₹72,000 to ₹1.02 lakh before any state top-up. Delhi and Uttar Pradesh add their own subsidy on top. Run your own numbers in the subsidy calculator.

On home charging, decisively. A Nexon EV at a tested 7.79 km/kWh on an ₹8/unit domestic tariff runs at about ₹1.17 per kilometre once you account for 88% charging efficiency. A 15 km/l petrol hatchback in Delhi at ₹102/litre runs at ₹6.81. The gap narrows sharply on public DC fast charging at ₹20–24/unit, which works out to roughly ₹2.73 per kilometre — still well under petrol, but it changes the payback on the price premium if you cannot charge at home. That last point is the one buyers underestimate.

Three things usually explain it. Your sanctioned load may be higher, and fixed charges are levied per kilowatt of sanctioned load regardless of what you consume. You may be on a different DISCOM or a different category with its own slab structure. And fuel surcharge and electricity duty are percentages — in Delhi the power purchase adjustment alone can add a third to the energy charge. The bill calculator itemises all of it.
